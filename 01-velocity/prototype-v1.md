## Show & Swap read

_What a partner understood from your build with no verbal setup, their reaction is your first piece of product evidence._

- **What they understood immediately:** Thought it was some sort of user dashboard for a business helping understand metrics
- **What confused them:** no title or header, it just went straight to numbers. The way it’s designed, it feels as if all the bottom healthy metrics make up the high warning number. Although after reading in more details they are just other stats to share and they are healthy but at first glance it felt like it was numbers that make up the top warning metric.
- **The assumption they thought you were testing:** ⁠that if you lead and share these numbers then a business user/employee will make the appropriate moves to correct and adjust how to operate the business
- **The gap between what you intended and what they read:** My goal was to create a dashboard that helps business users quickly identify the most important metric, understand why it matters, and decide what action to take. My partner understood that the dashboard was designed to help businesses interpret metrics, but they initially struggled with its presentation. They noticed the lack of a clear title or header and mistakenly interpreted the healthy supporting metrics as contributing to the main churn warning. The gap between my intention and their interpretation shows that prioritizing a metric visually does not necessarily make its meaning or relationship to other metrics clear. My next iteration would add a stronger page header, clarify why churn is the primary concern, and visually separate the supporting metrics so users can distinguish independent indicators from contributing factors.
