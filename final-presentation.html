Module 2 · The Frame

Problem & hypothesis

Problem

What was broken

The core analytics suite has a 60% bounce rate: 60% of sessions last under 15 seconds with no interaction. The data is accurate, but users can't find what to act on.

  • The default landing view shows 12 charts with equal weight.
  • The most-requested metric is 6 clicks away.

“I open it, see twelve charts, and have no idea which one I'm supposed to act on. So I close it.” (Marketing manager)

“The number I need is in there, but it takes six clicks and three filters to find it.” (Growth analyst)

*The metrics and quotes come from the project brief. They were not collected or checked in this project.*

Hypothesis (from the Module 2 Validation Brief):

Field
We believeReplace the 12-chart landing view with a dashboard highlighting the most important metric, a plain-language explanation, and a recommended action.
Will causeUsers identify actionable insights faster and are less likely to abandon the dashboard.
ForB2B SaaS product managers and growth leads who use the dashboard to make decisions.
We'll know we're right whenReduce the dashboard bounce rate from 60% to below 40% and have at least 70% of test users identify the key insight and recommended action within 30 seconds.
  • Riskiest assumption: showing the most important insight and a recommended action first will help users find actionable information faster and reduce dashboard abandonment.
  • Risk type: Usability (can they use it?)
  • Kill switch: if the dashboard bounce rate remains at or above 60% after introducing a guided headline and recommended action, investigate whether we're highlighting the wrong metric and pivot if necessary.

Validation status: not yet validated. The repo records no participant sessions. The V3 hypothesis-test prototype (`02-validation/v3/`) can measure the 30-second criterion and explicit abandonment in moderated tests, but its results table is still TBD. The bounce-rate criterion can only be measured with real traffic in production. This PRD treats the hypothesis as the bet being built toward, not a proven result.

Module 6 · The Evidence

Metric & evidence

What the data showed

Two testers successfully created three accounts and accessed the dashboard. Both reached the dashboard without reported navigation difficulties. A subsequent signup rate-limit error was observed.

Peer feedback: “Make the dashboard simpler.”

Engagement, return visits, task completion, and time to insight remain unmeasured.

Module 6 · The Move

Data signal → the one fix

The surgical change
ChangeHypothesisResult
Simplify the Executive Overview and Recommended Actions pages to emphasize the key churn insight and strategic recommendations.Simplifying the dashboard will help users identify the most important churn insight and recommended action more quickly, with less confusion.The redesigned dashboard was implemented, committed to GitHub, and successfully redeployed. All three pages passed functional checks. Improvements in engagement and time to insight have not yet been measured.
The Call

Recommendation

ITERATE, keep refining
The evidence behind the call

Decision: ☐ Go ☑ Iterate ☐ Kill

Both testers accessed the dashboard successfully, but feedback identified excessive complexity. The redesign addressed this feedback and was successfully deployed. Further user testing is needed to determine whether the changes improve engagement, task completion, and time to insight.

The Human Layer

My story, friction · learning · aha

Across six modules

Friction:

My biggest challenge was balancing informative content with simplicity, ensuring the dashboard provided meaningful insights without overwhelming users.

Learning:

I learned how AI can introduce scope drift by focusing on minor features and design preferences, consuming time without advancing the project's core objectives. I also gained practical experience with dashboard customization, Supabase integration, and user authentication.

Aha:

My biggest realization was how quickly AI can help build and deploy a functional dashboard with user authentication. However, a product manager cannot rely entirely on AI. They must maintain ownership of the project's direction, scope, priorities, and development timeline to ensure the product meets its objectives.
